# Website, meetings and online bill pay for a small water district

I'd use three pieces: a free .gov domain, a managed government website, and a water billing system with its own payment portal that the website links to. I couldn't verify any vendor's accessibility claims. Ask each one for a current accessibility report (a "VPAT") before you sign.

## The accessibility deadline

- **Deadline:** Some older pages still say 2027. On April 17, 2026, DOJ extended both compliance dates by one year. Smaller entities and special district governments now have until April 26, 2028.
- **Standard:** WCAG 2.1 Level AA remains the technical standard.
- **PDFs count:** Online documents used to deliver government services, including PDFs and forms, remain subject to WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Scanned-image agendas are a common failure.
- **Your payment vendor counts too:** Utilities cannot transfer ADA Title II liability to payment vendors by contract alone. The public entity remains responsible if a third-party platform fails to conform.

## Options I found

### Domain (free)
- **.gov via CISA:** .gov domains are only available to U.S.-based government organizations, and they're free. Special districts are eligible. You need sign-off from a senior official, so your board chair or general manager will have to approve it. Apply at https://get.gov/

### Website (low cost, managed)
- **Munibit:** Plans start at $99/month with no setup fees, no design fees, and no long-term contracts. That pricing is aimed at municipalities. Its association program page says counties and special districts should contact them for a custom quote. It lists a document library, forms, notifications, online payments, calendars and notices as built in. https://www.munibit.com/
- **Town Web:** Prices start at $99/mo, fixed for the contract term. It says sites are built to ADA and Section 508 standards, with accessibility scans and document scanning after launch. It describes itself as serving cities, towns, villages and townships, so ask whether it will take a water district. https://townweb.com/
- **WPPersona:** It markets to water districts, fire districts and other special districts with ADA-compliant websites. Its case study describes non-technical staff publishing water quality reports with PDF scanning built in. That case study is the vendor's own marketing, and I didn't find published pricing. https://wppersona.com/solutions/special-districts/
- **Enterprise vendors (CivicPlus and similar):** These are likely more than you need. One 2026 cost guide reports small towns paying $4,600 to $4,800 a year for the website alone on CivicPlus.

### Meetings and notices
For a small board, you probably don't need dedicated agenda software. Posting accessible agendas, minutes and notices as text-based PDFs or web pages on the site is enough to start. If you outgrow that, there are products built for this, such as CivicClerk, which one water district uses for accessible agendas (https://www.ylwd.com/about/board-meetings/), and eScribe (https://www.escribemeetings.com/markets/special-districts/).

### Online bill payment (water billing systems)
- **The Utility Biller:** It's a billing and customer management platform for small municipal and rural water systems, with plans from free to enterprise. The free plan adds a $2.00 convenience fee on online payments, and some paid plans remove it. Payments cover card, debit and ACH, powered by Stripe. https://theutilitybiller.com/
- **Spigot:** It's built for mutual water companies, community water systems and rural water districts. Customers can pay online by card or ACH, or keep paying by check. Data migration and training are included. I didn't find prices. https://usespigot.com/
- **Online Water Bill:** It covers billing and mailing, online and traditional payments, meter reading, QuickBooks Online, reminders and customer self-service. https://onlinewaterbill.com/
- **Missouri Rural Water's SUBS:** It's free but only for member systems with 1,000 or fewer active accounts, plus a $400 annual maintenance fee. This only helps if you're a Missouri Rural Water Association member. https://moruralwater.org/s-u-b-s-small-utility-billing-software/
- **Rural Billing:** Pricing starts at $2,850 one time. That listing is several years old, so treat the price as unconfirmed.

## Short list
1. **.gov domain:** https://get.gov/ (free).
2. **Website:** Munibit (https://www.munibit.com/). Get a written special-district quote, and get Town Web (https://townweb.com/) and WPPersona (https://wppersona.com/solutions/special-districts/) to quote as well.
3. **Billing and payments:** The Utility Biller (https://theutilitybiller.com/), with Spigot (https://usespigot.com/) as the comparison.

## What I'd pick
I'd get the **free .gov domain**, a **flat-fee managed government website** and **The Utility Biller** for billing. The website would link to the billing portal for payments.

- **Website:** I'd start with Munibit because it publishes a price and promises no contract or setup fee. Your own quote is the real test, since the published pricing isn't specifically for districts. If it won't serve a district, WPPersona is the next call because it explicitly targets water districts.
- **Billing:** The Utility Biller is the only one of the three billing products with published pricing, and it's built for small water systems with no IT staff. One decision for your board: the free tier's $2 fee is paid by your customers. Check whether your state or board policy allows passing on card fees. If not, ask about the paid plans.
- **Before signing with any vendor:**
  - Get a current VPAT and ask which parts of the product it covers.
  - Test the payment portal yourself with a keyboard only and with a screen reader.
  - Put an accessibility requirement in the contract.
- **Your own content:** The vendors can't fix this for you, so make sure every PDF you post is text-based.

I'd also run the vendor's sample site through a free checker such as WAVE and have your district's attorney confirm the deadline for your state.
