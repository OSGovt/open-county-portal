I'd get quotes from two or three managed, government-focused website vendors and keep billing as a separate, linked service. I haven't tested any of these products, and most of what I found is vendor marketing or third-party comparisons. Treat the claims as things to verify, and none of this is legal advice.

## The accessibility deadline
- On April 20, 2026, the DOJ published an interim rule extending the compliance dates for its 2024 Title II web accessibility rule.
- Public entities serving fewer than 50,000 people, and any special district government, now have until April 26, 2028.
- The standard, WCAG 2.1 Level AA, and the scope of covered content are unchanged.
- The DOJ also signaled it may do further rulemaking on the technical standards. Watch for that.
- The underlying nondiscrimination duties still apply now. I'd use the extra time to fix things rather than wait.
- Your meeting PDFs and your payment page both count. The rule's guidance covers third-party tools, so a bill-pay vendor's accessibility is your problem too.

## Websites (meetings, notices)
Prices vary a lot, so get written quotes.
- **CivicPlus Municipal Websites** is built for local governments. That includes special districts such as transit agencies and parks departments. Capterra lists a starting price of $39 a month, but real quotes run higher. One town was quoted $4,800 for the initial term and $4,722 a year after that. One comparison says Streamline and Municode, both now part of CivicPlus, target the smallest buyers, including special districts. [Capterra listing](https://www.capterra.com/p/10023577/CivicPlus-Municipal-Websites/)
- **Town Web** is a flat-fee option. It starts at $99 a month, fixed for the contract term. That figure comes from Town Web's own blog, so it's a vendor claim. [Pricing article](https://townweb.com/blog/how-much-does-a-municipal-website-cost/)
- **WPPersona** markets specifically to water and other special districts. Its case study describes a water district launching in 7 days, with PDF scanning built in for non-technical staff. It's vendor-written, and I found no independent reviews. [Special districts page](https://wppersona.com/solutions/special-districts/)
- **Custom WordPress** would cost more and needs ongoing upkeep. One agency estimates $6,000 to $18,000 to build, plus $150 to $400 a month for hosting and support. That's a lot to take on with no IT staff.

## Online bill payment
If you already have billing software, ask that vendor for a pay-online portal first. Otherwise:
- **The Utility Biller** is aimed at small water systems. It has plans from free to enterprise, with online payments, meter management, billing and a customer portal. On the free plan, a $2.00 convenience fee on online payments covers the cost, and some paid plans remove it. Payments are powered by Stripe. [theutilitybiller.com](https://theutilitybiller.com/)
- **Online Water Bill** is another small-system option. It covers billing, QuickBooks Online, reports and a customer payment portal. [onlinewaterbill.com](https://onlinewaterbill.com/)
- **Quikwater** is billing software for small rural and municipal utilities. It includes online bill pay and email billing. [quikwaters.com](https://quikwaters.com/)
- **Black Mountain Software** is built around small districts. It lets customers pay online, by text or phone, or over the counter. [blackmountainsoftware.com](https://blackmountainsoftware.com/utility-billing/)
- **Edmunds GovTech** is a larger, full-suite option. It supports both small water utility billing and large operations. [edmundsgovtech.com](https://www.edmundsgovtech.com/solutions/local-government-utilities/)

## A free domain
A **.gov domain** is a cheap credibility win. .gov domains are only available to U.S. government organizations, and they're free. Special districts are eligible. Apply at [get.gov](https://get.gov/domains/eligibility/). One source reported that CISA paused new requests in February 2026 because of a lapse in federal funding. I couldn't confirm the current status, so check the site. CISA also doesn't offer DNS hosting or host .gov websites or email, so your website vendor handles those.

## My pick
**Website:** I'd get quotes from CivicPlus (Municipal Websites or Streamline) and one or two cheaper vendors such as Town Web. CivicPlus has the longest track record, and its quotes are documented in public town records. Its pricing is real but not cheap, so I'd use the cheaper quotes as leverage.

**Billing:** If you have no online payment option today, I'd trial **The Utility Biller**. The free tier lets you test it with little risk, it's built for small water systems, and the vendor says it handles data migration. Decide up front whether customers or the district will cover the $2 fee.

**Before you sign anything, ask each vendor for:**
1. An Accessibility Conformance Report (VPAT/ACR) against WCAG 2.1 AA.
2. Whether the payment page, which is usually hosted by a third party, is covered by that report.
3. How meeting PDFs get checked for accessibility.
4. Whether you can export your content if you leave.

I couldn't verify accessibility for any of the billing products from the pages I found, so that's the biggest gap to close.
