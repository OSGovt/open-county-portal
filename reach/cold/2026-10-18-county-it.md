I'll search each of the three needs in parallel.**Short answer:** Open source can cover the website, the records search, and part of the payment front end. I found no open-source system for property tax billing or court-cost collection, so the payment piece will involve a vendor either way. I'd keep that vendor small and have it handle the card data.

## Deadline
The DOJ's ADA Title II rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA, and on April 20, 2026 the DOJ issued an interim final rule extending the compliance deadlines by one year. The new dates are April 26, 2027 for entities serving 50,000 or more people, and April 26, 2028 for smaller entities. A mid-sized county probably falls in the first group, which leaves about six months. Vendors don't remove the obligation either: hiring a third-party vendor does not transfer it. The rule also covers PDFs, online forms, and third-party platforms used to deliver public services.

## Options by need

**1. Website (WCAG 2.1 AA)**
- **LocalGov Drupal** (<https://new.drupal.org/localgov-drupal>) is a pre-configured Drupal. It bundles Drupal core, contributed modules, its own modules, themes and default configuration. Its community says all components are tested for accessibility compliance before inclusion. It also ships a localgov_sa11y feature that gives editors instant accessibility feedback.
- **Caveat:** it is built for councils in the UK and Ireland. Its content patterns (service pages, directories, events) carry over to a US county, but you'd be adapting it, not installing something US-specific. I didn't find a comparable US-county distribution, so I won't name one.
- Accessible themes don't make your content accessible. Your PDFs, forms and payment pages still have to be tested. Axe, Lighthouse and Silktide can verify compliance from a code and content perspective. NYC also publishes a WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility scanner for agency websites at <https://github.com/CityOfNewYork/accessibility-nyc>. I haven't evaluated it.

**2. Online payments**
- **Drupal Commerce** is open source and can sit inside the same Drupal site. It integrates with payment providers worldwide, with 142 contributed gateway modules. The gateway companies are proprietary, but you can keep card data off your server.
- Two integration styles matter:
  - **Hosted-form option:** the Authorize.Net Accept module (<https://www.drupal.org/project/commerce_authnet_accept>) sends card data directly to Authorize.Net and never reports it to Drupal. That module's page is nearly three years old, so check whether it's maintained before relying on it.
  - **Tokenized option:** the Stripe module is a tokenized payment gateway (<https://docs.drupalcommerce.org/v2/developer-guide/payments/gateways/>).
- The scope question matters for a one-person shop. A PCI white paper notes that in a shared-management approach, card information never touches the server running Drupal. That is the approach you want.
- **Hyperswitch** (<https://github.com/juspay/hyperswitch>) is an open source, composable payments platform with SaaS and self-host options. It is a payment orchestrator, not a government tax portal. Self-hosting it would likely put you in a heavy PCI scope, so I wouldn't use it at your staffing level. I only saw its repository listing, not its documentation.
- **The real gap is balances.** A portal needs to look up parcels and amounts owed. That data lives in your tax billing system for property tax, and in the court's case system for court costs. Neither is something I found an open-source replacement for. Courts are often run by the state judiciary, so confirm who owns that data and whether a statewide payment portal already exists. I didn't research that.
- **Non-open-source fallback:** Autoagent's MuniciPay (<https://autoagent.com/solutions/government/municipay/>) is advertised as free to government, with no setup, monthly, compliance, processing, chargeback, return, batch or networking fees. It is hosted and maintained by Autoagent and covers taxes and courts. That page is over a year old, so verify current terms. Whichever vendor you choose, require WCAG 2.1 AA conformance in the contract, because governments must update procurement and vendor contracts to require it.

**3. Records search**
"Records search" could mean two things.
- **Searching scanned or archived documents: Mayan EDMS** (<https://gitlab.com/mayan-edms/mayan-edms>). It is free and open-source, GPL-2.0-only. It offers search by text content, metadata or other attributes, with multiple search engines supported. It uses the Tesseract OCR engine by default. The docs say you can deploy the official Docker Compose file to get started. It is a staff and archive tool. I'm not sure its default interface meets WCAG 2.1 AA for the public, so I'd put a Drupal search page or an accessible front end over its REST API.
- **Public records requests (FOIA-style): NYC OpenRecords** (<https://github.com/CityOfNewYork/NYCOpenRecords>). It is a web app where the public submits requests and government employees manage, respond to and fulfill them. It is Apache-2.0, in Python, and has recent releases. It was built for New York's FOIL law, so your state's deadlines and exemptions would need custom work. I couldn't verify its accessibility. Most other products in this space (NextRequest, GovQA and similar) are proprietary SaaS.
- **Court-case search:** I found no open-source option. It depends on your court's case management system.

## What I'd install
1. **Drupal using the LocalGov Drupal distribution** for the website, with Sa11y and a recurring axe or Lighthouse scan. If your current CMS is workable, remediating it in place may beat a migration by April 2027 for a one-person shop.
2. **Drupal Commerce with a hosted-form gateway** for court costs and simple fees. Do this only if the court can give you a reliable feed of what is owed.
3. **For property tax, link out to a hosted payment page** from your tax system's vendor or a government-payments provider. Don't self-host balance lookups.
4. **Mayan EDMS** only if you have a real backlog of scanned records to make searchable. Otherwise, start with Drupal's built-in search.

**The one I'd pick:** LocalGov Drupal, because it is the only item above that fixes the legal deadline and the staffing problem together. The accessibility work is built in and shared with a community. The payment and records pieces can be added in stages without a vendor contract for the whole site. The cost is that you'll own patching and upgrades, and its UK orientation means some adaptation work. Before you commit, list which systems hold your tax and court balances, because that decides how the payment pieces get built.
